The '''Sony BRAVIA pre-Android Linux TVs''' are the 2011-2012 generation of Sony Bravia flat-panel televisions, which ran a proprietary Linux-based platform before Sony switched the BRAVIA line to Android TV. Hardware ranged from entry models to flagships with 12-bit X-Reality PRO video processing and active (shutter-glasses) 3D. All models included DLNA media playback and an HTML/JavaScript widget platform (AppliCast) served from Sony-operated servers.

==Consumer impact summary==
The hardware of this generation remains fully functional years after the platform was abandoned &mdash; including 12-bit video processing and active 3D on the higher-end models &mdash; but Sony discontinued every software pillar the sets depend on. Owners are left with smart functionality that no longer works through official channels, no official firmware recovery path for a failed or factory-reset set, GPL-licensed sources that are no longer obtainable from the copyright distributor, and a 3D playback feature that silently fails over DLNA.

Two characteristics distinguish this case from an ordinary end-of-support, and both can be checked against Sony's own servers today. First, the withdrawal is not a matter of files being lost: Sony's AppliCast content servers remain online in 2026 and still return the original application bundles with their original cryptographic signatures. What was switched off is the per-region catalog that tells a television those applications exist &mdash; a single XML file per country.<ref name="cat-eu">Sony Corporation, AppliCast application catalog for the AZ3 chassis, European region (live document): [https://applicast.ga.sony.net/WidgetContents/SNY_WidgetGallery/AZ3/Catalog_EU_ALL_eng.xml Catalog_EU_ALL_eng.xml]; archived copy: [https://web.archive.org/web/20260917210301/https://applicast.ga.sony.net/WidgetContents/SNY_WidgetGallery/AZ3/Catalog_EU_ALL_eng.xml Internet Archive snapshot]. Lists eight applications.</ref><ref name="cat-br">Sony Corporation, AppliCast application catalog for the AZ3 chassis, Brazilian region (live document): [https://applicast.ga.sony.net/WidgetContents/SNY_WidgetGallery/AZ3/Catalog_LA_BRA_por.xml Catalog_LA_BRA_por.xml]; archived copy: [https://web.archive.org/web/20260917204700/https://applicast.ga.sony.net/WidgetContents/SNY_WidgetGallery/AZ3/Catalog_LA_BRA_por.xml Internet Archive snapshot]. Lists two applications.</ref> Second, several applications never offered in some regions were nonetheless finished and fully localized for those regions, as Sony's own files show.

==Incidents==

===Smart services shut down, while the application files remain served===
The AppliCast widget platform depended on Sony-operated servers for its content. Those services were wound down and the sets lost their smart functionality through official channels. The content servers themselves, however, were never switched off: as of September 2026 <code>applicast.ga.sony.net</code> still answers and still returns the original application bundles. A catalog file for the preceding (2010) chassis generation, still served, carries an internal date of 2010-04-21.<ref name="az1">Sony Corporation, AppliCast catalog for the AZ1 chassis (live document, internal date 2010-04-21): [https://applicast.ga.sony.net/WidgetCatalogs/AZ1_EU_ALL_eng.xml AZ1_EU_ALL_eng.xml]; archived copy: [https://web.archive.org/web/20260917210817/https://applicast.ga.sony.net/WidgetCatalogs/AZ1_EU_ALL_eng.xml Internet Archive snapshot].</ref> The hardware is unaffected; what no longer exists is the catalog that directs a television to the content. Sony itself announced the end of individual services on its Brazilian support site, one notice per service, each naming the affected models &mdash; KDL-46EX725 and KDL-46HX855 among roughly a hundred and thirty sets. ===Applications withheld by regional catalog after localization was completed===
Comparing two Sony catalogs served from the same host, for the same chassis generation: the European catalog offers eight applications, the Brazilian catalog offers two.<ref name="cat-eu" /><ref name="cat-br" /> The applications not offered in Brazil were neither unfinished nor unlocalized. Four of them &mdash; Calculator, Alarm, Calendar and Analogue Clock &mdash; contain no language-dependent text at all, and their Sony-supplied manifests carry localized names in thirty languages, Portuguese among them.<ref name="calc">Sony Corporation, application manifest for the Calculator widget (live document), showing localized names in thirty languages including <code>&lt;name lang="por"&gt;Calculadora&lt;/name&gt;</code>: [https://applicast.ga.sony.net/WidgetBundles/SNY_BasicCalculator/info.xml SNY_BasicCalculator/info.xml]; archived copy: [https://web.archive.org/web/20260917204708/https://applicast.ga.sony.net/WidgetBundles/SNY_BasicCalculator/info.xml Internet Archive snapshot].</ref> The fifth, a World Clock, ships a Sony-supplied dictionary covering thirty-one languages in which the Portuguese entries are complete, including ''Rio de Janeiro'' in its list of timezone cities.<ref name="wcdic">Sony Corporation, World Clock application dictionary (live document), 31 languages; Portuguese entries include the weekday abbreviations and the timezone city list containing "Rio de Janeiro": [https://applicast.ga.sony.net/WidgetBundles/SNY_WorldClock/dic.txt SNY_WorldClock/dic.txt]; archived copy: [https://web.archive.org/web/20260917204715/https://applicast.ga.sony.net/WidgetBundles/SNY_WorldClock/dic.txt Internet Archive snapshot].</ref> The localization work was therefore completed and shipped to Sony's servers, but the applications were not listed for those markets, and the sets were abandoned in that state.

===GPL source releases removed from Sony's distribution service===
The sets run a Linux kernel and other GPL-licensed components. Sony operates a ''Source Code Distribution Service'' for exactly this purpose and describes the obligation in its own words on that site, which remains in service in 2026.<ref name="oss-live">Sony Corporation, "Source Code Distribution Service" (live): [https://oss.sony.net/Products/Linux/common/search.html oss.sony.net]; archived copy: [https://web.archive.org/web/20260901004026/https://oss.sony.net/Products/Linux/common/search.html Internet Archive snapshot]. States that source is provided for "Sony products with Linux and other open source software whose license requires the provision of source code", with physical-media copies available "for a period of three years after our last shipment of this product".</ref> Its television listings currently carry 798 <code>KDL-</code> models, none of them from the 2010&ndash;2012 EX/HX/NX/CX generation. An Internet Archive snapshot of the same Sony service from October 2014 shows that this generation '''was''' listed, naming models of it explicitly, including KDL-32/37/40/46/55/60EX725 and KDL-40/46/55HX855.<ref name="oss-2014">Internet Archive snapshot of Sony's Source Code Distribution Service, European TV category, 10 October 2014: [https://web.archive.org/web/20141010061250/https://oss.sony.net/Products/Linux/TV/category03.html web.archive.org]. Lists the 2010–2012 EX/HX/NX/CX models by model number.</ref> The archived download page for the group containing KDL-46EX725 lists twenty-three source packages &mdash; the kernel, the cross-compiler toolchain, the C library, the graphics stack and the browser engine, that is, the complete build environment.<ref name="oss-manifest">Internet Archive snapshot of the Sony source download page for the model group including KDL-46EX725, 27 July 2015: [https://web.archive.org/web/20150727011435/https://oss.sony.net/Products/Linux/TV/KDL-32CX520.html web.archive.org]. Lists <code>linux-kernel.tgz</code>, <code>sony-cross-gcc-for-dev-4.1.2</code>, <code>sony-target-dev-glibc-for-dev-2.7</code>, <code>sony-target-dev-directfb-1.3.0</code>, <code>WebCore.tar</code>, <code>JavaScriptCore.tar</code> and seventeen further packages.</ref> The download links preserved in that archived page return HTTP 404 on Sony's live server today, and the Internet Archive captured the index pages but not the files behind them. The televisions continue to run those binaries; the corresponding source is no longer obtainable from the distributor.

===Firmware self-service downloads ended; recovery routed through support===
Sony ended the self-service firmware downloads for these models. Its Brazilian support site now serves, in place of a download, a notice headed "Encerramento do software de atualização da TV" stating that software updates for certain older TV models have ended and are no longer available for download, with a model checker that returns KDL-46HX855 as affected and names its latest software as PKG2.120BRA.<ref name="eos-firmware">Sony Brasil, "Encerramento do software de atualização da TV" (end of TV update software), model-software page for KDL-46HX855: [https://www.sony.com.br/electronics/support/lcd-tvs-legacy-lcd-tv/kdl-46hx855/software/00269731 sony.com.br]. The page's model checker returns KDL-46HX855 as affected, latest version PKG2.120BRA, and states updates "encerraram e não estão mais disponíveis para download"; archived copy: [https://web.archive.org/web/20260918044450/https://www.sony.com.br/electronics/support/lcd-tvs-legacy-lcd-tv/kdl-46hx855/software/00269731 Internet Archive snapshot, 18 September 2026].</ref> Rather than a download, the page directs an owner of an affected model who needs the update to contact Sony's product-support team.<ref name="support-contact">Sony Brasil product-support contact form, archived 18 September 2026: [https://web.archive.org/web/20260918044211/https://br.esupport.sony.com/support/s/support-contact?language=pt_BR Internet Archive snapshot].</ref> A set that fails or is reset to factory defaults therefore has no self-service route back to working firmware; recovery depends on a discretionary support request, for which the page states no availability guarantee, or on third-party archives of the images Sony once published. The latest-version string on Sony's own checker, PKG2.120BRA, matches the final firmware recorded for this chassis by owner research.<ref name="repo">Owner research repository documenting the platform, the recovered manifests and the methods used to re-check every claim on this page: [https://github.com/danielcamposramos/sony-bravia-linux github.com/danielcamposramos/sony-bravia-linux].</ref>

===3D playback silently broken over DLNA===
The 3D-capable models of this line auto-engage 3D from exactly one signal: a ''frame_packing_arrangement'' SEI message (H.264 D.2.4, payload type 45) embedded in the H.264 elementary stream. They ignore the Matroska StereoMode container tag &mdash; the only 3D flag standard rip files carry &mdash; so every DLNA-served 3D video plays flat. The feature was never announced as ending; it fails silently on hardware that displays 3D perfectly from other inputs. The diagnosis was reverse-engineered by an owner and subsequently reviewed and accepted by an independent project: the HandBrake video transcoder merged a patch so that its encoder writes the missing signal, closing a long-standing issue in that project.<ref name="handbrake">HandBrake project, pull request #8100, "libhb: signal stereo 3d frame packing in the x264 encoder", merged 16 September 2026: [https://github.com/HandBrake/HandBrake/pull/8100 github.com/HandBrake/HandBrake/pull/8100]. Independent third-party review and acceptance of the technical finding described in this incident.</ref> Further reports were filed with the FFmpeg and mpv projects.<ref name="ffmpeg">FFmpeg issue tracker, issue 24530: [https://code.ffmpeg.org/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/issues/24530 code.ffmpeg.org], and issue 24531: [https://code.ffmpeg.org/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/issues/24531 code.ffmpeg.org].</ref> No DRM or copy-protection mechanism is involved in the restoration.

===3D display never available to the television's own internet/app content===
The 3D processing is a display feature of the panel, documented by Sony as applying to whatever the set shows. The on-device i-Manual describes a 3D button offering three display modes &mdash; Side-by-Side, Over-Under, and ''Simulated 3D'', the last converting ordinary 2D pictures to a simulated-3D effect &mdash; and states that the mode is chosen "to suit the displayed content", with an ''Auto 3D'' setting that engages 3D automatically "when a 3D signal is detected".<ref name="imanual-3d">Sony Corporation, KDL-46HX855 i-Manual (electronic manual built into the set), "Watching in 3D": document ID W0005741M (English) / W0005743M (Portuguese). Documents the [3D Display] modes Side-by-Side, Over-Under and Simulated 3D, the [Auto 3D] setting that switches automatically when a 3D signal is detected, and the [3D Signal Notification] setting.</ref> Crucially, the manual restricts only the two ''stereoscopic'' modes by input signal or format; it places no input, format or source restriction on Simulated 3D, whose only stated caveat is that the effect "may be less pronounced with some picture sources", and it states the one precondition that 3D is available only when pictures are shown full-screen.<ref name="imanual-3d" /> On a set playing video through the platform's own browser/application surface, full-screen, an owner observed that the television detected the 3D signal but would not switch to 3D &mdash; neither automatically nor by the manual 3D button &mdash; and that the documented 2D-to-3D Simulated 3D mode was likewise unavailable, although the identical panel switches to 3D from other inputs. The panel's documented 3D processing has therefore never been reachable from the internet/app content path that Sony built into the set and later shut down.<ref name="repo" />

==Consumer-rights context (Brazil)==
The two sets verified hands-on are Brazilian-market units, and the notices above were published by Sony Brasil. The following provisions of Brazilian consumer law may be relevant to owners in that market. This section states the statute and the matching, sourced facts; it draws no legal conclusion, which is a matter for the Foundation and for Brazilian consumer-law counsel.
*Under the ''Código de Defesa do Consumidor'' (Law 8.078/1990), advertising and product information provided by a supplier are binding and form part of the contract (art. 30). Sony Brasil published consumer material presenting "Internet TV" and "BRAVIA Internet Vídeo" as features of these sets;<ref name="br-promo">Sony Brasil consumer documents "Internet TV" (Sony doc W0001861M) and "BRAVIA Internet Vídeo" (W0001142M), presenting the internet-content capability of the BRAVIA line. Held in the owner's offline document library.</ref> those services were subsequently discontinued per Sony's own notices above.
*The CDC treats defects that render a product unfit for its purpose or diminish its value as ''vícios do produto'' (art. 18), and Brazilian courts have recognised supplier responsibility across a product's ''vida útil'' (useful life), which can extend beyond the formal warranty period.
*The CDC requires manufacturers and importers to ensure the availability of components and technical support while a product is offered and for a reasonable period after production ceases (art. 32).
*The CDC voids abusive contract clauses, including those incompatible with good faith or that place the consumer at exaggerated disadvantage (art. 51) &mdash; relevant where an end-user licence purports to permit unlimited discontinuation of advertised functionality.
*The distinction drawn elsewhere on this page holds here too: the discontinuation of online ''services'' is one question, while the removal of the GPL-licensed ''source code'' Sony's own licence documents point to is a separate obligation arising from the software licences themselves, independent of any service terms.

==Products==
Sony distributed software for these televisions '''per chassis generation, not per model''': one catalog and one set of application bundles served every set in a generation, and Sony's own service manuals cover several screen sizes under a single chassis. The incidents above therefore apply to the generation, not only to the models a researcher happened to own.
*'''AZ2-F chassis (2011)''' &mdash; a single Sony service manual covers KDL-32EX725, KDL-40EX725, KDL-46EX725 and KDL-55EX725, with KDL-60EX725 documented separately. 1080p, active 3D, DLNA, AppliCast widgets.
*'''AZ3F chassis (2012)''' &mdash; high-end line of the generation, including KDL-40/46/55HX855 and the HX853 series; 1080p, 12-bit X-Reality PRO processing, active 3D, DLNA, AppliCast widgets.
*'''AZ1 chassis (2010)''' &mdash; the preceding generation, whose catalog is also still served and still answers in 24 languages.<ref name="az1" />
*'''Verified hands-on''' &mdash; KDL-46EX725 (AZ2-F) and KDL-46HX855 (AZ3F).
