# Same panel, different paperwork — the regional documentation gap

The KDL-46HX855 (and its EX725 sibling) is one product. What Sony gave
the owner *about* it depended entirely on region. Established from our own
copies of the US and BR document sets, 2026-09-18.

## The asymmetry, measured

| region | what the owner got |
|---|---|
| **US** | Full **i-Manual** (`W0005741M`, ~9,700 lines) documenting every feature including the 3D display modes; a formal **EULA** (`44119951M`); a printed **Limited Warranty** (`41443860M`). |
| **BR** | Short **marketing / "Dicas de uso"** pages (10–88 lines each): "Internet TV" (`W0001861M`), "BRAVIA Internet Vídeo" (`W0001142M`), "Diferenças entre LCD, LED e TV 3D" (`W0003395M`), "TV Digital" (`W0003563M`), image-quality tips. |

Same hardware. The US owner received a complete operating reference; the
Brazilian owner received promotional explainers and setup tips. The
detailed feature documentation — the i-Manual that, for example, defines
exactly how Simulated 3D and Auto 3D behave — was not part of the
Brazilian consumer package in this set. This is the "Brazil is usually
underserved" pattern with primary-source receipts, not an impression.

## The Brazilian promotional documents promised the smart features

The BR pages are not neutral: they **advertise** the internet
capabilities to Brazilian buyers. "Internet TV" describes the current
BRAVIA models as having a feature that lets you access internet content
without a computer. "BRAVIA Internet Vídeo" walks the owner through
connecting the set to reach that content.

Those services are now dead — the same shutdown documented across this
repo.

## Why the Brazilian angle is legally different from the US one

This is factual framework, not legal advice; a Brazilian consumer-law
professional would have to assess any actual claim.

In the US, the EULA expressly reserved the right to discontinue services
"without notice and without liability" (see
[legal-eula-analysis.md](legal-eula-analysis.md)), which is why the
services shutdown is weak as a contract-breach argument there.

Brazil is a different jurisdiction with the **Código de Defesa do
Consumidor (CDC)**, under which, as a matter of public legal framework:

- **advertising and product information bind the supplier** (CDC art. 30
  — *a oferta vincula*): the "Internet TV" / "BRAVIA Internet Vídeo"
  material Sony Brasil published is the kind of information that art. 30
  treats as binding;
- products carry a **durability / useful-life expectation** and defects
  that impair them are *vícios* (CDC art. 18);
- **abusive contract clauses can be void** (CDC art. 51), including
  clauses that strip essential characteristics the product was sold with.

Whether the service shutdown, against Brazilian advertising and CDC
durability provisions, amounts to anything actionable is a question for a
Brazilian consumer-law professional — not a claim this document makes.
The point for the record is narrower and solid: **the same product was
marketed and documented differently by region, and the Brazilian owner
was both underserved on documentation and advertised services that were
later withdrawn.**
